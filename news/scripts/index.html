2026-10-26 · Call to Action #21

New Cree Nations Miyoyawin Centre completed: relevant context for Call to Action 21

TITLE: New Cree Nations healing centre opens — what Call to Action 21 still asks Estimated duration: 4–5 minutes DRAFT FOR CHRIS AND LEAH TO REVIEW BEFORE RECORDING Fact-checked against the October 26, 2026 joint release and the TRC's 2015 Calls to Action. CHRIS — INTRODUCTION A new First Nations-led treatment and healing facility has been completed in Saskatchewan. It's a concrete investment in culturally appropriate services. But what does it tell us about Canada's responsibility under the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's Calls to Action? Today we're looking at the facts, the progress, and the questions that still need answers. LEAH — VERIFIED NEWS On October 26, 2026, Indigenous Services Canada and Ahtahkakoop Cree Nation announced the completion of the Cree Nations Miyoyawin Centre. Community leaders marked its completion on October 22, on Ahtahkakoop Cree Nation in Treaty 6 Territory, Saskatchewan. The new centre replaces a facility built in 1988. According to the joint announcement, it supports residential treatment and culturally appropriate healing services for First Nations people facing substance use challenges in north-central Saskatchewan. CHRIS — FUNDING AND COMMUNITY LEADERSHIP The federal department reports investing 29.3 million dollars in the project. Ahtahkakoop Cree Nation contributed another 1.8 million dollars toward a dedicated detoxification space. That space is expected to become operational later; the announcement does not say it is already operating. The centre's board represents four First Nations: Ahtahkakoop, Big River, Pelican Lake, and Witchekan Lake. Its residential services are part of the National Native Alcohol and Drug Abuse Program treatment network. LEAH — EDUCATION: THE TRC CONNECTION Now, let's separate a welcome development from a claim that a Call to Action has been fulfilled. Call to Action 21 asks the federal government to provide sustainable funding for Indigenous healing centres addressing the physical, mental, emotional, and spiritual harms of residential schools. It also specifically prioritizes healing centres in Nunavut and the Northwest Territories. This Saskatchewan facility is relevant to the broader conversation about Indigenous-led healing. But the October 26 announcement does not establish that its funding is long-term operating funding under Call 21, that its services specifically address residential school harms, or that the call's northern priority has been met. CHRIS — HARD TRUTHS Here's the hard truth: a completed building is an important achievement, but a building announcement is not the same thing as evidence of sustained access, staffing, long-term funding, or healing outcomes. We should recognize what the four First Nations and their partners have accomplished without claiming more than the evidence supports. We also need to distinguish capital investment from the ongoing funding required to keep services available. LEAH — ACCOUNTABILITY Our first question for Indigenous Services Canada is straightforward: what ongoing operational funding is committed to the Cree Nations Miyoyawin Centre, and for how many years? Second, how will the government report whether the centre is meeting the needs identified by the communities it serves? Third, how does this investment fit into the federal response to Call 21, including the separate priority for Nunavut and the Northwest Territories? Those are questions for follow-up, not accusations. The October 26 release does not provide all those answers. CHRIS — WHAT WE CAN SAY TODAY We can verify that the new centre was completed, that federal and First Nation contributions were announced, and that the centre supports culturally appropriate residential treatment and healing services. We cannot use this single announcement to mark Call to Action 21 completed. LEAH — CLOSING For Delta Owl Eyes, accountability means recognizing real steps forward and asking what comes next. We'll track additional information about ongoing funding, service delivery, and community-reported results. When verified developments emerge, we'll produce a new report rather than rewrite this one.
