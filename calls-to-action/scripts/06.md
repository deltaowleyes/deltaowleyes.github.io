# Call to Action #6 — Repeal section 43 of the Criminal Code
Editorial draft · Original source: TRC Calls to Action (2015), Call #6
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today's focus is Call to Action number 6: repeal section 43 of the criminal code.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission called for the Government of Canada to repeal section 43 of the Criminal Code. This is a specific recommendation. We need to compare the government's actions with its actual requirements, not just the headlines.

The Commission's work documented the residential-school system and its continuing effects on children and families. For Indigenous communities, decisions about children's care and protection are connected to culture, family relationships, and dignity.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, I want to understand exactly what is being requested and who is responsible. We should separate the original recommendation from the debate about how to implement it.

**LEAH:**
**Education.** Call 6 asks for the Government of Canada to repeal section 43 of the Criminal Code. That is the standard against which progress must be assessed.

For families, this is about more than government language. It is about what people experience when they need help and whether decisions respect the people affected.

A policy can sound promising on paper. The questions are whether the required legal or institutional change has happened, how it works in practice, and what reliable evidence shows.

We should not treat Indigenous people as a single voice. First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities have different histories, legal relationships, and priorities. Our reporting must make room for those differences.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** A press release is not the same as implementation. Even when governments announce a related initiative, it may not satisfy every part of the Commission's recommendation.

That is why we will use dated sources and make clear what is confirmed and what remains uncertain.

**LEAH:**
Reconciliation needs accountability, but accountability must also respect community knowledge. Official documents can tell us what institutions say they have done. Families and Indigenous governments can help explain how decisions affect people.

We also have to be careful about the limits of a short video. We can explain the Call and identify questions to investigate. We cannot settle every legal, historical or community issue in a few minutes.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** Has Parliament enacted a repeal? Which official records establish the answer, and what arguments are being made about children's safety and family supports?

I also want to know who was consulted, how concerns were addressed, and where viewers can find the original records. Those questions are a starting point for reporting, not an accusation.

**LEAH:**
Our approach is to distinguish three things: what the Commission asked for, what institutions have actually done, and what results people are seeing.

When evidence changes, we'll publish a dated update. We won't silently turn an old claim into a new one. When the evidence is incomplete, we'll say so.

The important thing to remember about Call 6 is its specific request: the Government of Canada to repeal section 43 of the Criminal Code. We will keep returning to those words.

**CHRIS:**
That gives viewers a way to check our work, too. Follow the source, ask questions, and look for outcomes rather than promises alone.

**LEAH:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
