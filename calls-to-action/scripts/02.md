# Call to Action #2 — Publishing the child-welfare numbers
Editorial draft · Original source: TRC Calls to Action (2015), Call #2
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, and welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. We're continuing our examination of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's 94 Calls to Action. Today is number 2: publishing the child-welfare numbers.

When people talk about reconciliation, they often talk about promises. But the Truth and Reconciliation Commission gave Canada specific work to do. This Call is about annual public reporting about Indigenous children in care, comparisons with non-Indigenous children, reasons children are taken into care, prevention and care spending, and whether interventions work.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, one thing I'm learning is to distinguish the actual wording of a Call from the much broader political conversation around it. What does this Call require, and what evidence would show that the requirement has been met?

**LEAH:**
**Education.** Call number 2 focuses on annual public reporting about Indigenous children in care, comparisons with non-Indigenous children, reasons children are taken into care, prevention and care spending, and whether interventions work. That is the Commission's recommendation, not a claim by Delta Owl Eyes that a particular government has fulfilled it.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** Here's the distinction we need to remember: Annual reports are not just spreadsheets; they let families and communities ask whether policies are improving outcomes.

It's easy for a news report to stop at an announcement. The harder work is returning months or years later and asking what actually changed.

**LEAH:**
The Commission's recommendations came out of testimony and examination of the residential-school system and its continuing legacy. That history matters when evaluating present-day institutions. It also means our reporting must be careful not to speak for every Indigenous family or community.

At Delta Owl Eyes, we will distinguish the text of the Call, independently reported developments, and our own interpretation. We won't treat a funding commitment as a measured result. We won't describe a Call as completed just because one related action has occurred.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** Here are some questions worth putting to the responsible governments and organizations. Where are the latest comparable figures? Do reports separate First Nations, Inuit and Métis data appropriately? Are prevention spending and results published together?

Those questions are not accusations. They are a way of finding out whether public commitments have translated into meaningful change.

**LEAH:**
And we want to hear from people with direct experience, without assuming one person's experience represents everyone. We also want to hear from the governments and organizations responsible for implementation, and compare their answers with published evidence.

Our goal is education that leads to better questions, and accountability grounded in facts. When new evidence emerges, we'll report it as a dated update rather than quietly changing the history of what we said.

For now, remember what Call number 2 actually asks for: annual public reporting about Indigenous children in care, comparisons with non-Indigenous children, reasons children are taken into care, prevention and care spending, and whether interventions work. That's the standard against which progress should be measured.

**CHRIS:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes.

**LEAH:**
The public deserves figures that explain what is happening to children, not just what agencies spend. Families also deserve to know whether preventive supports are reaching them before separation occurs.

Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
