# Call to Action #68 — Commemorating the 150th anniversary of Confederation
Editorial recording draft · TRC Calls to Action (2015), #68
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call 68: commemorating the 150th anniversary of confederation.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission asks for the federal government, with Indigenous peoples and the Canadian Museums Association, to mark the 150th anniversary of Confederation in 2017 with funding for commemoration projects on reconciliation. We must start with that specific recommendation, rather than a government announcement that may address only part of it.

Canada's 150th anniversary in 2017 was an opportunity to ask whose histories national celebrations recognized. Call 68 proposed a dedicated way to support projects that would put reconciliation into public commemoration, not just anniversary speeches.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, I want to know whether the 2017 celebrations included a dedicated national funding program for reconciliation commemoration. A general anniversary budget is not automatically the program the Commission requested.

**LEAH:**
**Education.** The Call contains a specific 2017 anniversary and asks for funding of reconciliation-themed projects.

Commemoration can take many forms: an exhibition, a community history project, a public gathering or an educational installation. The key question is whether Indigenous peoples shaped what was remembered and how it was presented.

First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities are distinct. Their perspectives cannot be reduced to a single story. We should listen carefully without assuming that one account represents everyone.

The TRC specifically named three participants: the federal government, Indigenous peoples and the Canadian Museums Association. It asked them to mark the 2017 anniversary by establishing a dedicated national funding program for projects on the theme of reconciliation.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** A new program or official statement might be relevant, but it does not automatically meet every requirement. What was actually changed, and can the public verify it?

Because 2017 has passed, the evidence must be historical. Was a dedicated program established in time? What were its eligibility rules, budget and funded projects?

**LEAH:**
The people whose stories are being commemorated should have real authority over the projects. First Nations, Inuit and Métis experiences are not interchangeable, and a national funding program should not erase their distinct perspectives.

The original wording is important: the federal government, with Indigenous peoples and the Canadian Museums Association, to mark the 150th anniversary of Confederation in 2017 with funding for commemoration projects on reconciliation. If only one part of that requirement has been addressed, our reporting should say so.

We will not assign a current completion rating without a dated assessment.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** Where is the official record of the requested action? Who evaluates the results? What remains unfinished, and who must act next?

Can the federal government provide archived program criteria and a list of funded projects? What role did Indigenous partners and the Canadian Museums Association have in setting priorities?

**LEAH:**
We want to hear from Indigenous advocates and people with direct experience while respecting differences among communities. Their knowledge can reveal issues that are not visible in official summaries.

We will publish sources and make clear what is verified and what is our interpretation. When new information emerges, we will provide a dated update rather than quietly rewriting an earlier report.

The anniversary has passed, but the accountability question remains: did Canada establish the particular national reconciliation-commemoration funding program requested for 2017?

**CHRIS:**
We will keep comparing the original recommendation with documented action. Viewers should be able to check the source for themselves and ask informed questions.

**LEAH:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
