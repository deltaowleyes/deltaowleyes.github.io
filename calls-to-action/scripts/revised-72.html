HARD TRUTHS. EDUCATION. ACCOUNTABILITY.

Call 72 — A national residential school death register

Editorial recording draft — current implementation status requires separate dated verification.

LEAH:

Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call 72: a national residential school death register.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission asks for the federal government to allocate sufficient resources to the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation to develop and maintain the National Residential School Student Death Register. We must start with that specific recommendation, rather than a government announcement that may address only part of it.

The National Residential School Student Death Register is intended to preserve knowledge about children who died in the residential-school system. Maintaining an accurate register is an ongoing responsibility, not a one-time announcement.

CHRIS:

As an ally, I want to understand what resources the federal government has provided to the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation for this specific register, and whether those resources can sustain its work.

LEAH:

Education. The requirement is sustained resources for a maintained register, not simply an initial list.

Call 72 directs the federal government to allocate sufficient resources to the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation. The purpose is to allow the Centre to develop and maintain the National Residential School Student Death Register established by the Commission.

First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities are distinct. Their perspectives cannot be reduced to a single story. We should listen carefully without assuming that one account represents everyone.

Developing a register means carefully examining and reconciling available records. Maintaining it means having the staff, expertise and systems to review new information, correct errors and preserve records responsibly over time.

CHRIS:

Hard Truths. A new program or official statement might be relevant, but it does not automatically meet every requirement. What was actually changed, and can the public verify it?

How much dedicated funding has been allocated, over what period, and what ongoing support is available? A launch announcement does not establish that maintenance is adequately funded.

LEAH:

Families and Indigenous communities should be treated with respect when information is reviewed or updated. Accuracy matters, but so do privacy, cultural protocols and clear explanations of what remains uncertain.

The original wording is important: the federal government to allocate sufficient resources to the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation to develop and maintain the National Residential School Student Death Register. If only one part of that requirement has been addressed, our reporting should say so.

We will not assign a current completion rating without a dated assessment.

CHRIS:

Accountability. Where is the official record of the requested action? Who evaluates the results? What remains unfinished, and who must act next?

Can the federal government identify dedicated resources for the register? Can the Centre describe what sustained maintenance requires, and whether existing support meets those needs?

LEAH:

We want to hear from Indigenous advocates and people with direct experience while respecting differences among communities. Their knowledge can reveal issues that are not visible in official summaries.

We will publish sources and make clear what is verified and what is our interpretation. When new information emerges, we will provide a dated update rather than quietly rewriting an earlier report.

Call 72 is specifically about adequate federal resources for a register that can be developed and maintained. Its progress should be assessed using funding evidence and the Centre's documented needs.

CHRIS:

We will keep comparing the original recommendation with documented action. Viewers should be able to check the source for themselves and ask informed questions.

LEAH:

This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
