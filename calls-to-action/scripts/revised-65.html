HARD TRUTHS. EDUCATION. ACCOUNTABILITY.

Call 65 — Research on reconciliation

Editorial recording draft — current implementation status requires separate dated verification.

LEAH:

Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call 65: research on reconciliation.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission asks for the federal government, through the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council and with Indigenous peoples, post-secondary institutions and the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation, to establish a national research program advancing understanding of reconciliation. We must start with that specific recommendation, rather than a government announcement that may address only part of it.

Research about reconciliation must not treat Indigenous communities merely as subjects of study. The people whose histories and futures are being researched should help define the questions, methods and benefits.

CHRIS:

As an ally, I want to know whether federal research funding supports work defined with Indigenous partners, rather than research priorities imposed on them.

LEAH:

Education. This Call requires multi-year funding, a national research program and collaboration with educators and partner institutions.

A national research program could examine how reconciliation is understood in schools, communities and public institutions. But its value depends on whose knowledge is respected and whether findings are accessible to the people involved.

First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities are distinct. Their perspectives cannot be reduced to a single story. We should listen carefully without assuming that one account represents everyone.

Call 65 specifically names the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council, Indigenous peoples, post-secondary institutions and the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation. It calls for a national research program with multi-year funding. That partnership and sustained support are not optional details.

CHRIS:

Hard Truths. A new program or official statement might be relevant, but it does not automatically meet every requirement. What was actually changed, and can the public verify it?

One grant or a short-term project is not automatically the national, multi-year program the Commission requested. What program was established, for how long, and with which partners? And how can Canadians find its budget, governance structure and results? I want to see clear evidence that Indigenous partners helped set the agenda and that the work continues beyond an initial announcement.

LEAH:

Indigenous researchers, Elders and communities bring knowledge that institutions have too often excluded. Meaningful collaboration means shared influence over research priorities, not just an invitation to comment after decisions are made.

The original wording is important: the federal government, through the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council and with Indigenous peoples, post-secondary institutions and the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation, to establish a national research program advancing understanding of reconciliation. If only one part of that requirement has been addressed, our reporting should say so.

We will not assign a current completion rating without a dated assessment.

CHRIS:

Accountability. Where is the official record of the requested action? Who evaluates the results? What remains unfinished, and who must act next?

Can the federal government and the research council identify multi-year funding commitments, Indigenous governance roles and publicly available research outcomes?

LEAH:

We want to hear from Indigenous advocates and people with direct experience while respecting differences among communities. Their knowledge can reveal issues that are not visible in official summaries.

We will publish sources and distinguish verified evidence from interpretation. When new information emerges, we will provide a dated update rather than quietly rewriting an earlier report.

The accountability question is not simply whether reconciliation research exists. It is whether a sustained national program was created collaboratively, as Call 65 requests.

CHRIS:

We will keep comparing the original recommendation with documented action. Viewers should be able to check the source for themselves and ask informed questions.

LEAH:

This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
