# Call to Action #71 — Residential school death records
Editorial recording draft · TRC Calls to Action (2015), #71
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call 71: residential school death records.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission asks for chief coroners and provincial vital statistics agencies that have not provided records to the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation to make available records on deaths of Indigenous children in residential schools. We must start with that specific recommendation, rather than a government announcement that may address only part of it.

Records of children who died while under residential-school authorities' care are part of the truth families and communities have sought for generations. Call 71 identifies specific public agencies holding potentially important evidence.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, I want to know which chief coroners and provincial vital statistics agencies still hold relevant records, and whether they have made those records available to the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation.

**LEAH:**
**Education.** The Call requires relevant coroners and vital statistics agencies to provide existing records.

This Call does not ask agencies to create a new register. It asks agencies that had not supplied their records to the Commission to make documents about deaths of Indigenous children in residential-school authorities' care available to the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation.

First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities are distinct. Their perspectives cannot be reduced to a single story. We should listen carefully without assuming that one account represents everyone.

Chief coroners and provincial vital statistics agencies are the named institutions. A public statement of cooperation is not the same as confirmation that relevant records have actually been identified and transferred or made accessible to the Centre.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** A new program or official statement might be relevant, but it does not automatically meet every requirement. What was actually changed, and can the public verify it?

Which agencies have responded, what categories of records were provided, and are there documented gaps? Those questions require careful evidence rather than assumptions about individual families.

**LEAH:**
Families and communities deserve clear information about how records are handled and what remains unknown. Any public reporting should protect sensitive personal details and respect Indigenous protocols while pursuing the truth.

The original wording is important: chief coroners and provincial vital statistics agencies that have not provided records to the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation to make available records on deaths of Indigenous children in residential schools. If only one part of that requirement has been addressed, our reporting should say so.

We will not assign a current completion rating without a dated assessment.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** Where is the official record of the requested action? Who evaluates the results? What remains unfinished, and who must act next?

Can each responsible agency document its response to Call 71? Has the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation confirmed receipt or access, and what outstanding records has it identified?

**LEAH:**
We want to hear from Indigenous advocates and people with direct experience while respecting differences among communities. Their knowledge can reveal issues that are not visible in official summaries.

We will publish sources and make clear what is verified and what is our interpretation. When new information emerges, we will provide a dated update rather than quietly rewriting an earlier report.

Call 71 is about a defined records-sharing obligation. We must ask which agencies complied and what documentary evidence supports that assessment.

**CHRIS:**
We will keep comparing the original recommendation with documented action. Viewers should be able to check the source for themselves and ask informed questions.

**LEAH:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
