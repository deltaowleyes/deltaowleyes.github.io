# Call to Action #14 — Indigenous Languages Act
Editorial draft · Original TRC Calls to Action (2015), #14
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today's focus is Call fourteen, the Indigenous Languages Act.

The Commission called on the federal government to enact language legislation incorporating five principles. They concern the urgency of preserving languages, Treaty rights, sufficient federal funding, Indigenous community leadership, and funding that reflects linguistic diversity.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, I want to understand how the five principles appear in the law. What can viewers check in official records? And how do we know whether implementation is working?

**LEAH:**
**Education.** Indigenous languages carry stories, knowledge, relationships, and cultural teachings. Residential schools disrupted their transmission between generations.

The Commission did not simply request a law with a promising title. It named five expectations. Indigenous languages should be valued and urgently preserved. Treaty relationships reinforce language rights. Federal resources must be sufficient. Communities should lead revitalization. Funding must reflect the diversity of languages.

Those principles are connected, but they are not identical. A program might support one language while leaving another without resources. A consultation might occur without giving communities meaningful authority.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** Passing legislation and fulfilling its principles are different milestones. We should examine how funding decisions are made, who participates, and whether communities consider the resources adequate.

**LEAH:**
That distinction matters for Indigenous families. Revitalization requires teachers, speakers, learners, and long-term community commitment. A short-term announcement cannot by itself show whether a language is thriving.

Our reporting should separate the original recommendation, official action, and evidence about results. We should also avoid treating First Nations, Inuit, and Métis languages as though they all face identical circumstances.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** Which principles are reflected in legislation and how is funding evaluated? Are community priorities shaping decisions? Is the funding transparent, predictable, and appropriate for different language situations?

**LEAH:**
We will not declare the Call complete based on an announcement alone. We need dated records and perspectives from people doing the language work.

Language revitalization is about more than a government checklist. It concerns the ability to pass knowledge to children and future generations. Indigenous communities should have authority in that work.

For Call fourteen, keep the five principles in view. Ask not just whether legislation exists, but whether its design and implementation reflect what the Commission requested.

**CHRIS:**
We'll link the original source so viewers can check our reporting. When new evidence arrives, we'll publish a dated update.

**LEAH:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
