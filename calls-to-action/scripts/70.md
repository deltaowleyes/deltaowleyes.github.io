# Call to Action #70 — Review of Canadian archival policies
Editorial recording draft · TRC Calls to Action (2015), #70
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call 70: review of canadian archival policies.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission asks for the federal government to fund the Canadian Association of Archivists to review archival policies and best practices with Indigenous peoples for compliance with the UN Declaration and the Joinet-Orentlicher principles. We must start with that specific recommendation, rather than a government announcement that may address only part of it.

Archives hold evidence about residential schools. How those records are kept, described and made available affects the ability of Indigenous peoples to learn what happened and why.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, I want to know whether archival institutions have reviewed their actual policies, not just announced that they support reconciliation.

**LEAH:**
**Education.** The Call is a funded, collaborative review and recommendations for archives, not merely a digitization project.

Call 70 asks the federal government to fund the Canadian Association of Archivists for a national review conducted with Indigenous peoples. That is different from Call 69, which addresses Library and Archives Canada's own responsibilities.

First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities are distinct. Their perspectives cannot be reduced to a single story. We should listen carefully without assuming that one account represents everyone.

The review must evaluate compliance with the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples and the Joinet-Orentlicher Principles concerning the right to know the truth about residential-school human rights violations. It must also produce recommendations for full implementation across Canadian archives.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** A new program or official statement might be relevant, but it does not automatically meet every requirement. What was actually changed, and can the public verify it?

Where is the national review, what archives participated, and what methods were used? A digitization project is useful, but it is not a substitute for examining policies and publishing recommendations.

**LEAH:**
Indigenous peoples must be partners in assessing how archival systems serve their communities. The review should consider access to records, respectful description, preservation and the ability to correct incomplete or misleading accounts.

The original wording is important: the federal government to fund the Canadian Association of Archivists to review archival policies and best practices with Indigenous peoples for compliance with the UN Declaration and the Joinet-Orentlicher principles. If only one part of that requirement has been addressed, our reporting should say so.

We will not assign a current completion rating without a dated assessment.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** Where is the official record of the requested action? Who evaluates the results? What remains unfinished, and who must act next?

Did the federal government fund the requested work? Did the Canadian Association of Archivists publish recommendations? Which institutions have demonstrated changes against those recommendations?

**LEAH:**
We want to hear from Indigenous advocates and people with direct experience while respecting differences among communities. Their knowledge can reveal issues that are not visible in official summaries.

We will publish sources and make clear what is verified and what is our interpretation. When new information emerges, we will provide a dated update rather than quietly rewriting an earlier report.

Call 70 requires evidence of a funded, Indigenous-collaborative national review and a report recommending full implementation of the named international principles.

**CHRIS:**
We will keep comparing the original recommendation with documented action. Viewers should be able to check the source for themselves and ask informed questions.

**LEAH:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
