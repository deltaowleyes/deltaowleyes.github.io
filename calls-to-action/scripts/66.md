# Call to Action #66 — Funding for Indigenous youth reconciliation
Editorial recording draft · TRC Calls to Action (2015), #66
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call 66: funding for indigenous youth reconciliation.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission asks for the federal government to establish multi-year funding for community-based youth organizations delivering programs on reconciliation and a national network to share information and best practices. We must start with that specific recommendation, rather than a government announcement that may address only part of it.

Young people are not just the next generation to inherit reconciliation. They are already building relationships, asking difficult questions and leading community initiatives. This Call recognizes that youth organizations need dependable resources to keep that work going.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, I want to ask a practical question: can a youth organization plan a reconciliation program beyond one summer or one grant cycle? The federal government is specifically asked to fund that kind of continuity.

**LEAH:**
**Education.** The recommendation specifically supports community-based youth organizations and a national knowledge-sharing network.

A youth-led workshop, language gathering or exchange can bring people together, but organizing it takes staff time, safe spaces, transportation and materials. Short-term funding can make long-term relationships difficult to sustain.

First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities are distinct. Their perspectives cannot be reduced to a single story. We should listen carefully without assuming that one account represents everyone.

There are two distinct commitments in Call 66: multi-year funding for community-based youth organizations to deliver reconciliation programs, and a national network where organizations can share information and best practices. Funding without the network would address only part of the Call.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** A new program or official statement might be relevant, but it does not automatically meet every requirement. What was actually changed, and can the public verify it?

A government press release may announce youth funding, but how many years does it cover? Is it available to community-based organizations, and does it explicitly support reconciliation programs?

**LEAH:**
Young people should help decide what a useful program looks like. A national network should also allow organizations in smaller and remote communities to share lessons without being overshadowed by larger institutions.

The original wording is important: the federal government to establish multi-year funding for community-based youth organizations delivering programs on reconciliation and a national network to share information and best practices. If only one part of that requirement has been addressed, our reporting should say so.

We will not assign a current completion rating without a dated assessment.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** Where is the official record of the requested action? Who evaluates the results? What remains unfinished, and who must act next?

Where is the public record of multi-year commitments, which organizations received support, and how does the national network operate? Can youth groups actually join and exchange practices, or is the network only a proposal?

**LEAH:**
We want to hear from Indigenous advocates and people with direct experience while respecting differences among communities. Their knowledge can reveal issues that are not visible in official summaries.

We will publish sources and make clear what is verified and what is our interpretation. When new information emerges, we will provide a dated update rather than quietly rewriting an earlier report.

Call 66 asks Canada to invest in youth reconciliation work over time and connect organizations across the country. We should measure both commitments separately.

**CHRIS:**
We will keep comparing the original recommendation with documented action. Viewers should be able to check the source for themselves and ask informed questions.

**LEAH:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
