# Call to Action #49 — Faith groups rejecting colonial doctrines
Editorial recording draft · TRC Calls to Action (2015), #49
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call 49: faith groups rejecting colonial doctrines.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission asks for religious denominations and faith groups that have not already done so to repudiate the Doctrine of Discovery and terra nullius. We must start with that specific recommendation, rather than a government announcement that may address only part of it.

The residential-school system and its legacy are central to the Commission's work. These Calls ask institutions to confront that history and make meaningful changes.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, I want to understand who is responsible, which official records show action, and what Indigenous communities say about the results. We should not confuse a promise with a demonstrated outcome.

**LEAH:**
**Education.** This request focuses on formal repudiation of concepts historically used to justify European sovereignty over Indigenous lands and peoples.

Faith groups must formally reject the Doctrine of Discovery and terra nullius, which were used to rationalize European sovereignty over Indigenous lands. This is distinct from apologizing for residential schools. The Call asks denominations that have not done so to repudiate these doctrines.

We should check actual denominational resolutions and their dates, rather than assuming every religious organization has taken the same position.

Indigenous communities should have a voice in assessing whether the work respects their rights and distinct legal traditions.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** A new program or official statement might be relevant, but it does not automatically meet every requirement. What was actually changed, and can the public verify it?

We should examine the dates of the evidence and the responsibilities of different institutions. If sources disagree, we need to explain that uncertainty rather than guessing.

**LEAH:**
Reconciliation requires institutions to listen to people affected by their decisions. Communities should have meaningful opportunities to shape programs and evaluate whether they work.

The original wording is important: religious denominations and faith groups that have not already done so to repudiate the Doctrine of Discovery and terra nullius. If only one part of that requirement has been addressed, our reporting should say so.

We will not assign a current completion rating without a dated assessment.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** Where is the official record of the requested action? Who evaluates the results? What remains unfinished, and who must act next?

Which specific documents can viewers inspect, and which institution is responsible for the next step?

**LEAH:**
We want to hear from Indigenous advocates and people with direct experience while respecting differences among communities. Their knowledge can reveal issues that are not visible in official summaries.

We will publish sources and make clear what is verified and what is our interpretation. When new information emerges, we will provide a dated update rather than quietly rewriting an earlier report.

Call 49 deserves its own assessment. Its specific requirements should not disappear behind broad promises about reconciliation.

**CHRIS:**
We will keep comparing the original recommendation with documented action. Viewers should be able to check the source for themselves and ask informed questions.

**LEAH:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
