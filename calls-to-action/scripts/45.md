# Call to Action #45 — A Royal Proclamation of Reconciliation
Editorial recording draft · TRC Calls to Action (2015), #45
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call 45: a royal proclamation of reconciliation.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission asks for the Government of Canada to jointly develop with Indigenous peoples a Crown-issued Royal Proclamation of Reconciliation, building on the 1763 Royal Proclamation and 1764 Treaty of Niagara. Our review must compare the full original recommendation with documented action and Indigenous perspectives.

The residential-school system and its legacy are central to the Commission's work. These Calls ask institutions to confront that history and make meaningful changes.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, I want to understand who is responsible, which official records show action, and what Indigenous communities say about the results. We should not confuse a promise with a demonstrated outcome.

**LEAH:**
**Education.** The proposed proclamation must repudiate the Doctrine of Discovery and terra nullius, implement the UN Declaration, renew Treaty relationships and reconcile legal orders.

The proposed Royal Proclamation would build on the Royal Proclamation of 1763 and the Treaty of Niagara of 1764. It would renew nation-to-nation relationships and reject the Doctrine of Discovery and terra nullius, concepts used to justify taking Indigenous lands.

The Commission also calls for reconciliation between Indigenous and Crown legal orders, including Indigenous laws in Treaty and land-claim processes. A ceremonial proclamation without those commitments would miss important parts of the recommendation.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** A new program or official statement might be relevant, but it does not automatically meet every requirement. What was actually changed, and can the public verify it?

We should examine the dates of the evidence and the responsibilities of different institutions. If sources disagree, we need to explain that uncertainty rather than guessing.

**LEAH:**
That is why the exact scope of this Call matters. The original wording is important: the Government of Canada to jointly develop with Indigenous peoples a Crown-issued Royal Proclamation of Reconciliation, building on the 1763 Royal Proclamation and 1764 Treaty of Niagara. If only one part of that requirement has been addressed, our reporting should say so.

We will not assign a current completion rating without a dated assessment.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** Where is the official record of the requested action? Who evaluates the results? What remains unfinished, and who must act next?

Which records can the public inspect? Who is responsible for the next step?

**LEAH:**
We want to hear from Indigenous advocates and people with direct experience while respecting differences among communities. Their knowledge can reveal issues that are not visible in official summaries.

We will publish sources and make clear what is verified and what is our interpretation. When new information emerges, we will provide a dated update rather than quietly rewriting an earlier report.

Call 45 deserves its own assessment. Its specific requirements should not disappear behind broad promises about reconciliation.

**CHRIS:**
We will keep comparing the original recommendation with documented action. Viewers should be able to check the source for themselves and ask informed questions.

**LEAH:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
