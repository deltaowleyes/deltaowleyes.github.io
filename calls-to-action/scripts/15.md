# Call to Action #15 — An Indigenous Languages Commissioner
Editorial draft · Original source: TRC Calls to Action (2015), Call #15
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, and welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're examining Call to Action number 15: an indigenous languages commissioner.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission called for the federal government to appoint an Indigenous Languages Commissioner in consultation with Indigenous groups. That is our starting point. A related announcement is not necessarily evidence that the recommendation has been fulfilled.

Language and identity connect generations. Residential schools disrupted families and the transmission of Indigenous languages, names, and cultural knowledge. These Calls address parts of that legacy.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, I want to ask questions without speaking for Indigenous communities. How was the commissioner selected? Where can viewers find the office's work and its assessments of federal funding?

**LEAH:**
**Education.** The commissioner should promote Indigenous languages and report on whether federal funding for language initiatives is adequate. Appointment and performance are different questions.

We must also recognize that First Nations, Inuit, and Métis communities are distinct. Languages differ, as do local priorities and the relationships communities have with governments.

When reporting on this Call, we should distinguish what the Commission requested, what institutions have done, and what people are experiencing. Those are not interchangeable.

An official announcement may be relevant, but it must be checked against the original requirement. We will not declare a Call completed based on a headline alone.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** An office can exist while language workers still lack resources. Public reporting on funding adequacy is a concrete responsibility.

The public deserves evidence about implementation, not just a promise. And community perspectives can reveal gaps that official summaries miss.

**LEAH:**
For Indigenous families, this work is about more than a government checklist. It concerns the ability to carry knowledge, language, and identity forward with dignity.

People doing this work need to be heard in decisions about priorities and resources. We should not assume that an institution's description of success is the only valid measure.

We also have to be clear about what we don't know. This episode explains the recommendation. A separate dated report is needed to establish current implementation status.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** What public reports assess funding adequacy? How were Indigenous groups consulted, and what responses have governments given?

These are questions for public institutions and for our reporting, not accusations against individuals.

**LEAH:**
We will listen to people with direct experience while respecting that no single person speaks for every community.

For Call number 15, remember the precise requirement: the federal government to appoint an Indigenous Languages Commissioner in consultation with Indigenous groups. That is what we must keep checking.

When evidence changes, we'll publish a dated update and explain the source. We won't silently rewrite what was recorded before.

**CHRIS:**
We should check the specific responsibility, the available records, and the experiences of those affected. That's how we keep our work accountable: follow the original Call, check the evidence, and ask what remains.

**LEAH:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
