Call #6 — Repeal section 43 of the Criminal Code

Editorial recording draft — legislative status, timing and speaking balance require final verification.

LEAH:

Boozhoo, and welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call to Action number six. It is short, but its implications deserve careful discussion.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission called on the Government of Canada to repeal section 43 of the Criminal Code of Canada.

Section 43 is a provision concerning the use of force by parents and people acting in the place of parents, including teachers, when correcting a child. Its application has been limited by court decisions. The Commission nevertheless called for its repeal.

CHRIS:

This is a good example of why we must check the original wording. Call six is not a general recommendation about education funding. It is a specific recommendation to change federal criminal law.

LEAH:

Education. The history behind this Call matters. Residential schools were places where Indigenous children experienced coercive discipline and were separated from their families, languages, and communities. The Commission's recommendations address that legacy, including how Canada thinks about children's dignity and protection.

We should be precise: repealing section 43 is the action the Commission requested. It would not, by itself, solve every problem facing children, and we shouldn't claim that it would.

But the legal rule is important because laws communicate what conduct is permitted and what protections children can expect.

CHRIS:

Hard Truths. A Call to Action can be just one sentence and still demand substantial political action. Public discussion can also become confusing when people debate wider parenting issues without addressing the actual legal provision.

LEAH:

For Indigenous families, this conversation should never be reduced to stereotypes or claims that one community speaks with a single voice. Children deserve safety, and families deserve respectful, practical support.

It is also important to separate three questions: What did the Commission recommend? What does the law currently say? And has Parliament taken the specific action requested?

Those questions require different evidence. The first is answered by the original TRC document. The second requires checking the current Criminal Code and relevant court decisions. The third requires reviewing legislation and its status.

We will not describe this Call as completed or unfinished based on an old headline alone. Before making a current status claim, we need dated, authoritative legislative records.

CHRIS:

Accountability. Has Parliament repealed section 43? If a bill has been introduced, where is it in the legislative process? What reasons have governments given for supporting or opposing repeal?

LEAH:

And how are Indigenous organizations, child advocates, families, and young people included in the discussion? What resources support nonviolent, culturally respectful approaches to caring for children?

These are questions for public institutions, not accusations against individual families.

The Commission's Call is clear. It asks the federal government to repeal section 43. Our job is to report accurately on whether that specific change happens, and to explain the wider historical context without overstating what any single law can accomplish.

CHRIS:

That's Call to Action number six.

LEAH:

We'll keep following the evidence, the legislation, and the people affected. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
