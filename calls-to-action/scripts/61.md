# Call to Action #61 — Funding for community healing and reconciliation
Editorial recording draft · TRC Calls to Action (2015), #61
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call 61: funding for community healing and reconciliation.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission asks for church parties to the settlement agreement, with Indigenous collaboration, to establish permanent funding for community-controlled healing, culture and language revitalization, education and relationship-building projects. We must start with that specific recommendation, rather than a government announcement that may address only part of it.

The residential-school system and its legacy are central to the Commission's work. These Calls ask institutions to confront that history and make meaningful changes.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, I want to understand who is responsible, which official records show action, and what Indigenous communities say about the results. We should not confuse a promise with a demonstrated outcome.

**LEAH:**
**Education.** This requires permanent funding for Indigenous-directed projects, not a one-time donation.

Church parties are asked to establish permanent funding in collaboration with Survivors and Indigenous organizations. The funding is for community-controlled healing and reconciliation, cultural and language revitalization, and education and relationship-building.

A fourth part is easy to overlook: regional dialogue where Indigenous spiritual leaders and youth can discuss spirituality, self-determination and reconciliation. Permanent support is different from an occasional donation.

Indigenous voices should guide how the results are assessed, not merely appear in a final announcement.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** A new program or official statement might be relevant, but it does not automatically meet every requirement. What was actually changed, and can the public verify it?

We should examine the dates of the evidence and the responsibilities of different institutions. If sources disagree, we need to explain that uncertainty rather than guessing.

**LEAH:**
Reconciliation requires institutions to listen to people affected by their decisions. Communities should have meaningful opportunities to shape programs and evaluate whether they work.

The original wording is important: church parties to the settlement agreement, with Indigenous collaboration, to establish permanent funding for community-controlled healing, culture and language revitalization, education and relationship-building projects. If only one part of that requirement has been addressed, our reporting should say so.

We will not assign a current completion rating without a dated assessment.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** Where is the official record of the requested action? Who evaluates the results? What remains unfinished, and who must act next?

Can communities decide how funds are used? What continuing support exists for youth dialogue, language, healing and community-directed education?

**LEAH:**
We want to hear from Indigenous advocates and people with direct experience while respecting differences among communities. Their knowledge can reveal issues that are not visible in official summaries.

We will publish sources and make clear what is verified and what is our interpretation. When new information emerges, we will provide a dated update rather than quietly rewriting an earlier report.

Call 61 deserves its own assessment. Its specific requirements should not disappear behind broad promises about reconciliation.

**CHRIS:**
We will keep comparing the original recommendation with documented action. Viewers should be able to check the source for themselves and ask informed questions.

**LEAH:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
