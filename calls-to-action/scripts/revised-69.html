Call 69 — Library and Archives Canada reconciliation work

Editorial recording draft — current implementation status requires separate dated verification.

LEAH:

Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call 69: library and archives canada reconciliation work.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission asks for Library and Archives Canada to fully adopt and implement the UN Declaration and the UN Joinet-Orentlicher principles on the preservation of archives relating to human rights violations. We must start with that specific recommendation, rather than a government announcement that may address only part of it.

For Survivors and families, records can help establish what happened and why. Archives are not simply shelves of documents; decisions about preserving, describing and opening records can shape access to truth.

CHRIS:

As an ally, I want to know whether someone researching a residential school can actually find relevant records at Library and Archives Canada, understand the catalogue and request access without unnecessary barriers.

LEAH:

Education. The Call also requires accessible residential school records and greater resources for public education materials and programming.

Call 69 contains three duties. First, Library and Archives Canada must implement the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples and the Joinet-Orentlicher Principles in relation to the right to know the truth about residential-school human rights violations.

First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities are distinct. Their perspectives cannot be reduced to a single story. We should listen carefully without assuming that one account represents everyone.

Second, its residential-school holdings must be accessible to the public. Third, it must devote more resources to public education materials and programming about residential schools. A new webpage alone cannot demonstrate all three.

CHRIS:

Hard Truths. A new program or official statement might be relevant, but it does not automatically meet every requirement. What was actually changed, and can the public verify it?

What proportion of relevant holdings is described and available, and what barriers remain? Are access restrictions explained clearly, with appropriate protection for sensitive personal information?

LEAH:

Indigenous peoples should help determine how records are described, preserved and shared. The right to know the truth matters alongside respectful handling of information about Survivors, children and families.

The original wording is important: Library and Archives Canada to fully adopt and implement the UN Declaration and the UN Joinet-Orentlicher principles on the preservation of archives relating to human rights violations. If only one part of that requirement has been addressed, our reporting should say so.

We will not assign a current completion rating without a dated assessment.

CHRIS:

Accountability. Where is the official record of the requested action? Who evaluates the results? What remains unfinished, and who must act next?

Can Library and Archives Canada show policy changes tied to both international frameworks, evidence of improved public access, and a documented increase in education resources? Where are those reports, budgets and access measures published?

LEAH:

We want to hear from Indigenous advocates and people with direct experience while respecting differences among communities. Their knowledge can reveal issues that are not visible in official summaries.

We will publish sources and make clear what is verified and what is our interpretation. When new information emerges, we will provide a dated update rather than quietly rewriting an earlier report.

Call 69 is not complete merely because archives exist. We need evidence for all three duties: implementing rights-based principles, making holdings accessible, and strengthening public education.

CHRIS:

We will keep comparing the original recommendation with documented action. Viewers should be able to check the source for themselves and ask informed questions.

LEAH:

This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
