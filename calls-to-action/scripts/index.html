Living-room newsroom scripts

Five research-grounded two-host episodes for Chris and Leah, designed for a stationary camera with the river and Bkejwanong / Walpole Island visible through the window. Scripts are drafts for review before recording, not claims of independent verification by Delta Owl Eyes.

Recording setup

Position both presenters slightly angled toward each other, with the window off to one side rather than directly behind them. Lock camera exposure on faces to avoid silhouetting. Keep the river visible as context, but avoid showing private homes or identifiable neighbours without permission. Record 5 seconds of quiet room sound; use captions, a simple lower-third with call number and source, and a short pause between speakers.

Editorial standard: Read the cited primary call and current status page before recording. Label third-party statuses as third-party assessments; verify any recent changes on recording day. Do not present draft scripts as published investigations.

CALL #1 · TWO-HOST SCRIPT · APPROX. 75–95 SECONDS

Child welfare: what does reducing children in care require?

Chris: In 2015, the Truth and Reconciliation Commission called for fewer Indigenous children in care. But what would real progress look like?

Leah: Call to Action One has five requirements. They include monitoring neglect investigations, supporting families to stay together safely, and training child-welfare decision-makers about residential schools.

Chris: Indigenous Watchdog classifies this call as stalled. Its September 2026 review also documents policy and funding developments, including an Ontario child-welfare agreement.

Leah: That distinction matters. A new agreement may represent progress, but it does not by itself prove that every requirement has been met.

Chris: Our accountability question: where are the consistent national measures showing fewer children in care and better support for families?

Leah: We're Delta Owl Eyes, reporting from Bkejwanong. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability.

CALL #14 · TWO-HOST SCRIPT · APPROX. 75–95 SECONDS

Indigenous languages: law versus lived revitalization

Chris: Canada passed the Indigenous Languages Act in 2019. Does that mean Call to Action Fourteen is finished?

Leah: Not necessarily. The TRC called for legislation grounded in principles including language rights, adequate funding and preservation.

Chris: The federal government cites the 2019 law as progress. Indigenous Watchdog still classifies Call Fourteen as stalled.

Leah: These assessments are looking at different questions: whether legislation exists, and whether its full promises are being realized.

Chris: Our question is what communities can measure: stable language funding, fluent speakers, teachers and opportunities for children to learn.

Leah: For Delta Owl Eyes, language revitalization is not just a policy headline. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability.

CALL #66 · TWO-HOST SCRIPT · APPROX. 75–95 SECONDS

Youth reconciliation programs: is funding sustained?

Chris: Reconciliation isn't only about looking backward. Call to Action Sixty-Six is about investing in young people now.

Leah: The TRC called for multi-year federal funding for community-based youth organizations delivering reconciliation programs, and a national network to share knowledge.

Chris: Indigenous Watchdog currently classifies this call as in progress.

Leah: But in progress raises an important question: are community organizations receiving predictable support that lasts beyond one announcement or grant cycle?

Chris: We want to examine funding agreements, program continuity and what youth organizers themselves say is working.

Leah: From the river at Bkejwanong, this is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability.

CALL #80 · TWO-HOST SCRIPT · APPROX. 75–95 SECONDS

A federal holiday is an action, not the whole story

Chris: September thirtieth is now the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation, a federal statutory holiday established in 2021.

Leah: That responds to Call to Action Eighty, which asked for a statutory day to honour Survivors, families and communities, and support public commemoration.

Chris: Indigenous Watchdog classifies Call Eighty as complete. The federal government also identifies the holiday as an achievement.

Leah: Completion of one call does not mean reconciliation itself is complete.

Chris: The next question belongs to all of us: how do we carry that learning and accountability into the other 364 days of the year?

Leah: This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability.

CALL #92 · TWO-HOST SCRIPT · APPROX. 75–95 SECONDS

Businesses and Indigenous rights: beyond a statement

Chris: What does reconciliation require from businesses operating on Indigenous lands?

Leah: Call to Action Ninety-Two asks the corporate sector to adopt the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples as a reconciliation framework.

Chris: It also calls for meaningful consultation and free, prior and informed consent, equitable economic opportunities, and training for management and staff.

Leah: Indigenous Watchdog classifies this call as not started. That is its assessment of the call as a whole, not a claim that no business has taken action.

Chris: Our question to companies is simple: where are your public policies, agreements, training records and measurable outcomes?

Leah: Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability.
