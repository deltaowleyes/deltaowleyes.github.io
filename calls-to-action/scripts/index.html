Every Call. A Newsroom Script.

Two-host scripts for Chris and Leah covering context, requirements, Hard Truths, responsibility, evidence, Bkejwanong relevance and reader action. Approximate reading times appear on each script.

Editorial notice: These are production drafts, not completed investigations. Original TRC wording and independent status assessments require verification before recording. Some profiles lack complete requirement breakdowns. Before recording, verify the original wording, call number, title, status date, and every factual assertion. Scripts sharing a general structure are not substitutes for original reporting. National findings should not be presented as verified local experiences. Filming: Place the river-facing window off-axis to avoid silhouettes, record clean audio, and add dated source captions.
Pre-recording quality checklist
  1. Compare the episode with the original TRC Calls to Action PDF.
  2. Confirm the named institution is responsible for the specific requirement.
  3. Check independent progress classifications and their assessment dates; do not treat them as Delta Owl Eyes findings.
  4. Add at least one dated, call-specific example and a source caption before publishing.
  5. Ask community contributors for permission before attributing local perspectives.
  6. Time a rehearsal; cut repetition before recording.

Before the 94: Who We Are, Why We Are Here

Chris & Leah · 504 spoken words · approximately 3.7 minutes at 135 words/minute · Leah 71.4% / Chris 28.6%. Editorial draft; delivery timing and source review pending.

Read the revised introduction script

LEAH: Boozhoo, and welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. I'm Leah, a member of Walpole Island First Nation, Bkejwanong. We're starting a series about Canada's Truth and Reconciliation Commission and its 94 Calls to Action.

The Calls to Action came out of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's work examining the residential school system and listening to survivors. They ask governments, institutions, and people across Canada to take specific steps toward reconciliation.

For me, this history touches families and communities, and the future we're building for our children.

CHRIS: I'm Chris. I'm not First Nations; I'm here as an ally, a learner, and Leah's co-host. We won't pretend to speak for every Indigenous person or community. Our job is to listen, research, and ask clear questions. This series is part of my learning, too, and I take that seriously. We'll distinguish evidence from opinion, and give people room to disagree respectfully.

LEAH: Education. There are 94 Calls, covering child welfare, education, language and culture, health, justice, reconciliation, and more. Each one deserves more than a headline.

We'll explain what the original recommendation says, who is responsible, what reliable sources tell us has happened, and what questions remain unanswered.

You'll hear me speak from my own Indigenous perspective, and Chris will bring an ally's questions. Neither perspective replaces the voices of survivors, Elders, or other First Nations, Inuit, and Métis communities.

CHRIS: And we'll give you links to the original Calls and the evidence behind our reports. If we make a mistake, we'll correct it clearly. A completion claim needs evidence, not just a headline.

LEAH: Hard Truths. An announcement isn't the same as action. A funding commitment isn't the same as a result. And a completed project doesn't necessarily mean an entire Call to Action is complete.

We also want to celebrate Indigenous knowledge, culture, businesses, and community initiatives. Reconciliation is not only a discussion of what went wrong. It also concerns whose voices guide what happens next.

Our website, deltaowleyes.ca, brings together the Calls to Action library, news updates, and community resources. We want it to be useful to everyone.

CHRIS: Accountability. For every Call, we'll ask: What exactly was promised? Who is responsible? What evidence is available? And who is still waiting for answers? We'll distinguish a proposal from a funded program, and a funded program from real outcomes.

LEAH: When something changes, we'll publish a dated update rather than quietly rewriting the past. When evidence is incomplete, we'll say so. When an issue needs community knowledge or a survivor's perspective, we'll approach it with respect.

You don't need to know all 94 Calls before joining this conversation. We'll take them one at a time, in plain language, with sources you can check yourself.

Our hope is that viewers come away understanding both the history and the work that remains. You can learn alongside us, question our reporting, and hold public institutions to the commitments they've made.

CHRIS: Welcome to the series. Let's start with Call number one.

LEAH: This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
