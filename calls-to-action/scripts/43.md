# Call to Action #43 — UN Declaration as a reconciliation framework
Editorial recording draft · TRC Calls to Action (2015), #43
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call 43: un declaration as a reconciliation framework.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission asks for federal, provincial, territorial and municipal governments to fully adopt and implement the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples as the framework for reconciliation. Our review must compare the full original recommendation with documented action and Indigenous perspectives.

The residential-school system and its legacy are central to the Commission's work. These Calls ask institutions to confront that history and make meaningful changes.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, I want to understand who is responsible, which official records show action, and what Indigenous communities say about the results. We should not confuse a promise with a demonstrated outcome.

**LEAH:**
**Education.** This Call includes municipal governments and requires implementation, not just endorsement.

When governments adopt the UN Declaration, the test is not whether they display its name on a website. It is whether decisions about land, language, culture and participation reflect its principles. Federal, provincial, territorial and municipal authorities all have responsibilities under this Call.

A federal measure cannot by itself prove that a municipality has changed its approach. We should ask each government to identify its own policies, implementation dates and ways Indigenous nations helped shape the decisions.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** A new program or official statement might be relevant, but it does not automatically meet every requirement. What was actually changed, and can the public verify it?

We should examine the dates of the evidence and the responsibilities of different institutions. If sources disagree, we need to explain that uncertainty rather than guessing.

**LEAH:**
That is why the exact scope of this Call matters. The original wording is important: federal, provincial, territorial and municipal governments to fully adopt and implement the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples as the framework for reconciliation. If only one part of that requirement has been addressed, our reporting should say so.

We will not assign a current completion rating without a dated assessment.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** Where is the official record of the requested action? Who evaluates the results? What remains unfinished, and who must act next?

Which records can the public inspect? Who is responsible for the next step?

**LEAH:**
We want to hear from Indigenous advocates and people with direct experience while respecting differences among communities. Their knowledge can reveal issues that are not visible in official summaries.

We will publish sources and make clear what is verified and what is our interpretation. When new information emerges, we will provide a dated update rather than quietly rewriting an earlier report.

Call 43 deserves its own assessment. Its specific requirements should not disappear behind broad promises about reconciliation.

**CHRIS:**
We will keep comparing the original recommendation with documented action. Viewers should be able to check the source for themselves and ask informed questions.

**LEAH:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
