# Call to Action #67 — Review of museum policies
Editorial recording draft · TRC Calls to Action (2015), #67
https://nctr.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf

**LEAH:**
Boozhoo, welcome to Delta Owl Eyes. Today we're looking at Call 67: review of museum policies.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission asks for the federal government to fund the Canadian Museums Association to review museum policies and best practices, in collaboration with Indigenous peoples, for compliance with the UN Declaration and make recommendations. We must start with that specific recommendation, rather than a government announcement that may address only part of it.

Museums decide which histories visitors encounter and how cultural belongings are described. For Indigenous communities, a museum policy can affect who has authority to interpret an object, share knowledge, or request its return.

**CHRIS:**
As an ally, I want to know what rules museums follow when they hold Indigenous cultural belongings. Are communities consulted before an exhibit opens, and can they challenge descriptions that misrepresent their history?

**LEAH:**
**Education.** The review should address museum policies and best practices, not just a single exhibit.

A respectful exhibition matters, but Call 67 goes deeper. The review should examine museum-wide policies: collecting, interpretation, access, stewardship and relationships with the peoples whose heritage is represented.

First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities are distinct. Their perspectives cannot be reduced to a single story. We should listen carefully without assuming that one account represents everyone.

The federal government is asked to fund the Canadian Museums Association to conduct a national review in collaboration with Indigenous peoples. That review must assess compliance with the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples and produce recommendations.

**CHRIS:**
**Hard Truths.** A new program or official statement might be relevant, but it does not automatically meet every requirement. What was actually changed, and can the public verify it?

Where is the national review, who participated, and what recommendations were made? Funding a consultation is not the same thing as completing a review and making its findings available.

**LEAH:**
First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples have distinct histories and cultural protocols. Collaboration cannot mean that museums make every decision first and then ask communities to endorse it. The review should consider whether Indigenous peoples have meaningful influence over museum practices.

The original wording is important: the federal government to fund the Canadian Museums Association to review museum policies and best practices, in collaboration with Indigenous peoples, for compliance with the UN Declaration and make recommendations. If only one part of that requirement has been addressed, our reporting should say so.

We will not assign a current completion rating without a dated assessment.

**CHRIS:**
**Accountability.** Where is the official record of the requested action? Who evaluates the results? What remains unfinished, and who must act next?

Did the federal government provide the requested funding? Did the Canadian Museums Association publish a national assessment against the Declaration? Which recommendations can museums and the public examine today?

**LEAH:**
We want to hear from Indigenous advocates and people with direct experience while respecting differences among communities. Their knowledge can reveal issues that are not visible in official summaries.

We will publish sources and make clear what is verified and what is our interpretation. When new information emerges, we will provide a dated update rather than quietly rewriting an earlier report.

The test for Call 67 is specific: a funded, collaborative national museum-policy review, measured against the Declaration, with recommendations that people can read and assess.

**CHRIS:**
We will keep comparing the original recommendation with documented action. Viewers should be able to check the source for themselves and ask informed questions.

**LEAH:**
This is Delta Owl Eyes. Hard Truths. Education. Accountability. Miigwech for watching.
