INVESTIGATIONS / ACCOUNTABILITY · October 25, 2026

TRC Accountability Audit: 14 of 94 Calls to Action Complete

Education, justice and health: a desk-based comparison of published progress assessments.

Key finding: The reviewed Indigenous Watchdog tracker classifies 14 of 94 calls complete. Only one of the 39 calls in the three sectors examined here is classified complete. Statuses reflect the tracker’s criteria and should not be confused with proof of broader social outcomes.

Executive assessment

Indigenous Watchdog reported 14 of 94 Calls to Action complete in its March 1 and September 30, 2026 snapshots—approximately 15%. The September snapshot, reported in the press, listed 41 in progress, 24 stalled and 15 not started. This is a comparison of published assessments, not an independent verification of implementation. The Assembly of First Nations (AFN) September 2025 report card uses different criteria and indicated approximately 13 cumulative completions.

Sector comparison

Using non-overlapping sector definitions, education (Calls 6–12 and 62–65) includes 11 calls, with none classified complete; justice (Calls 25–42 and 50–52) includes 21, with one complete (Call 41); and health (Calls 18–24) includes seven, with none complete. Across these 39 calls, the reviewed Indigenous Watchdog call-level classifications show one complete, 18 in progress, 12 stalled and eight not started. These individual classifications were retrieved October 25, 2026 and should not be assumed to be dated September 30.

National status and arithmetic

On March 1, 2026 the reported breakdown was 14 complete, 39 in progress, 25 stalled and 16 not started. The combined stalled-or-not-started total is 41 of 94, or 43.6%, rather than the 45% headline appearing on the tracker homepage. On September 30 the press-reported breakdown was 14 complete, 41 in progress, 24 stalled and 15 not started. That yields 39 of 94 (41.5%) stalled or not started. The share therefore **decreased between these two 2026 snapshots**, although the tracker cited a 37% baseline from June 2025. The baseline was not independently verified.

Education: no completed calls

The reviewed call-level ledger classifies Calls 6, 9, 10 and 64 as not started; Calls 8, 62 and 63 as stalled; and Calls 7, 11, 12 and 65 as in progress. The AFN's September 2025 assessment graded both education categories “Little progress.” It identified unresolved education funding and infrastructure gaps, including unfunded regional education agreements. The proposed repeal of Criminal Code section 43 under Bill C-273 did not become law before Parliament was prorogued in January 2025.

Justice: one technical completion

Call 41, which called for a public inquiry into missing and murdered Indigenous women and girls, is classified technically complete because the National Inquiry delivered its final report in 2019. That classification does not mean its resulting 231 Calls for Justice have been implemented. Calls 26, 34, 51 and 52 were classified not started; Calls 25, 27, 28, 29, 30 and 35 stalled; and Calls 31, 32, 33, 36, 37, 38, 39, 40, 42 and 50 in progress. The AFN graded both justice categories “Little progress.” The Indigenous Justice Strategy was released in March 2025, but the AFN had not identified new implementation funding in its September 2025 assessment. Indigenous Watchdog reported that Alberta's Bill 13 contributed to downgrades for Calls 27 and 28 in December 2025.

Health: activity without completion

The reviewed ledger classifies Calls 18, 19 and 24 as stalled and Calls 20, 21, 22 and 23 as in progress. None is classified complete. The AFN graded health “Moderate progress,” reflecting developments including professional association apologies and policy activity. This is not a contradiction: the AFN evaluates category-level progress while Indigenous Watchdog tracks whether individual calls meet completion criteria. Call 19's specified measurable health-gap goals and annual reporting remain especially important for assessing outcomes.

Accountability gaps

The recurring-reporting requirements of Calls 9 (education), 19 (health outcomes) and 30 (Indigenous overrepresentation in custody) are all classified stalled or not started. Without the reporting specified in those calls, readers cannot readily evaluate whether policies and funding announcements translate into measurable change. Alberta's Bill 13 was cited by Indigenous Watchdog in its January 2026 reporting as a factor in downgrading Calls 27, 28 and 63. These are tracker judgments, not independent legal findings by Delta Owl Eyes.

Recommendations

Governments and institutions should publish transparent annual outcome data for Calls 9, 19 and 30; identify implementation funding alongside announced strategies; distinguish institutional milestones from improvements in people's lives; address jurisdictional barriers affecting professional training and curriculum commitments; and publicly respond to outstanding recommendations relating to missing children and unmarked burials. These are Delta Owl Eyes editorial recommendations drawn from the cited assessments.

The 14 calls classified complete

Indigenous Watchdog's reviewed list identifies Calls **13, 15, 41, 49, 53, 59, 67, 68, 70, 80, 83, 84, 85 and 94**. They address language rights and a languages commissioner; the MMIWG inquiry; church responsibilities; the National Council for Reconciliation; museum and archival work; commemoration; media initiatives; and the citizenship oath. Inclusion here records the tracker's classification only. In particular, supporting delivery evidence for Calls 49 and 68 was not independently checked for this audit.

Method and limitations

This desk review compares Indigenous Watchdog's March and September 2026 national snapshots, call-level classifications retrieved October 25, 2026, and AFN report cards from September 2025 and June 2024, against the official TRC Calls to Action. The AFN and Watchdog assess different dimensions of progress, so their counts should not be treated as interchangeable. Call-level pages did not show a uniform assessment date. The September national totals came from secondary press reporting. The audit did not independently inspect implementation records, budgets or community outcomes. Readers should interpret all statuses as attributed assessments rather than settled facts.
